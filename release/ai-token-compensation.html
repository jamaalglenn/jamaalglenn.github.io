TechCrunch Features Jamaal Glenn's Argument on AI-Token Compensation

March 22, 2026

NEW YORK - TechCrunch featured Jamaal Glenn in a report by Connie Loizos examining whether AI-token allowances are becoming a fourth form of engineering compensation or another cost of doing business.

The story described companies treating compute access as a recruiting benefit alongside salary, equity, and bonuses. Glenn focused on what this framing can hide: a token allowance is useful for work, but it does not transfer value to an employee in the way cash or ownership does.

“What may seem like a perk can be a clever way for companies to inflate the apparent value of a compensation package without increasing cash or equity - the things that actually compound for an employee over time.”

TechCrunch also quoted Glenn's distinction between compute and durable compensation: “Your token budget doesn't vest. It doesn't appreciate. It doesn't show up in your next offer negotiation the way a base salary or equity grant does.”

That difference matters if employers normalize tokens as pay. A growing compute allowance can be described as investment in employees while cash compensation stays flat. It can also carry a productivity expectation: if a company spends the equivalent of another salary on compute, finance teams will eventually ask how that changes the required headcount.
