Zero G Talent Cites Jamaal Glenn on the Hidden Risks of AI-Token Pay

JUNE 10, 2026

NEW YORK - Zero G Talent cited Jamaal Glenn in a June 10 analysis by David Yu of the financial risks engineers face when companies count AI-token allowances as part of total compensation.

The article examines offers that combine a $375,000 salary with $100,000 in compute credits and present the package as $475,000 in total compensation. It argues that the comparison breaks down because the credits do not vest, appreciate, or carry into a future negotiation the way salary history or equity can.

Yu writes that Glenn "laid out the distinction bluntly: token budgets lack every structural feature that makes equity valuable." The article contrasts restricted stock units, which can vest over time and appreciate with company growth, with compute credits that disappear when employment ends.

That distinction creates a structural mismatch. The company can book tokens as a real operating cost, while an employee may be encouraged to treat the same amount as durable personal compensation. Only the first treatment is guaranteed to survive the year in which the compute is consumed.

The article places the issue inside a wider labor-market contradiction: executives expect AI to reduce headcount while competing for scarce AI talent. For candidates, the practical question is not whether compute helps them work. It is whether the offer accurately separates a productivity tool from cash, equity, and wealth they can retain.
