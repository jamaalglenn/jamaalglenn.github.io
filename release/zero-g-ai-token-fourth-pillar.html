Zero G Talent Cites Jamaal Glenn on AI Tokens as a Fourth Pay Pillar

JUNE 11, 2026

NEW YORK - Zero G Talent cited Jamaal Glenn in a June 11 analysis by Elena Petrova of compute credits as an emerging fourth pillar of technology compensation alongside salary, bonus, and equity.

The article traces how access to AI compute has moved from an internal tooling decision into recruiting and offer negotiations. It cites examples ranging from candidates asking for guaranteed daily token budgets to employers treating large compute allocations as a way to attract engineers who can turn access into greater output.

Petrova also identifies the limitation at the center of Glenn's argument: token budgets "don't vest, don't appreciate, and don't carry forward into future negotiations the way base salary or equity grants do." A $100,000 allocation can be economically useful in the year it is consumed without adding $100,000 to an employee's net worth.

The article frames the issue as a CFO-level concern. Companies need to decide how much compute to fund, how to measure the productivity it produces, and whether describing it as compensation will create retention problems when employees compare it with assets they can own.

Its forward-looking proposal is to design around that mismatch, potentially by linking sustained compute use to tenure milestones or equity. The larger point is that AI infrastructure is becoming part of the talent market, but employers and candidates still need an honest vocabulary for what belongs in a pay package.
