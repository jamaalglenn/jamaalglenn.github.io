Executive

Jamaal Glenn is chief financial officer at SoLo Funds and founder and CEO of JG Holdings, an investment holding company and advisory firm. He is an executive, investor, entrepreneur, writer, speaker, and board director working at the intersection of finance and technology.

At SoLo Funds, Glenn manages enterprise AI inference budgets and has built and managed AI agents across finance, operations, and go-to-market. His writing and public commentary focus on AI inference economics, AI compensation, corporate AI adoption, labor-market effects, and board oversight. His analysis has been featured by TechCrunch and L'Express.

Earlier in his career, he worked across investment banking, venture capital, media, and technology, including roles at J.P. Morgan, Google, Alumni Ventures, First Look Media, and Schmidt Futures. He founded and sold digital-media businesses, raised more than $150 million for venture capital funds, and led more than $75 million in acquisitions and venture investments. He previously taught finance, marketing, and entrepreneurship at New York University and the City University of New York.

Glenn is a 2025-2026 Obama Foundation USA Leader, a Marshall Memorial Fellow, and a member of the Council on Foreign Relations and the National Committee on U.S.-China Relations. He holds an MBA from Stanford Graduate School of Business, an MPA from Harvard Kennedy School, and bachelor's degrees in finance and journalism from the University of Missouri.

Corporate Board Director

Jamaal Glenn is an executive, investor, and board director whose governance work centers on financial oversight, enterprise risk, capital allocation, and consequential decisions at moments of change.

He chairs the board of The Pivot Fund and serves as a board director, treasurer, and audit committee chair at LION Publishers. He also serves on the board of the Center for Public Integrity.

His board perspective comes from operating and investing experience across fintech, venture capital, media, and technology. As chief financial officer at SoLo Funds, Glenn brings current experience in financial discipline, corporate AI adoption, and the questions boards should ask about technology costs, productivity claims, labor effects, and management accountability.

Trisector Leader

Jamaal Glenn is a 2025-2026 Obama Foundation USA Leader, a Marshall Memorial Fellow of the German Marshall Fund, and a Salzburg Global Fellow. These fellowships connect his operating and investing work with a broader record of public leadership and international exchange.

He is also a member of the Council on Foreign Relations and the National Committee on U.S.-China Relations, networks that bring together leaders working across business, policy, academia, and civil society.

Thought Leader

Jamaal Glenn is a writer and commentator on the business decisions shaping finance, technology, and work. He writes Jamaal's Letter and publishes essays and analysis on AI inference economics, AI compensation, corporate adoption claims, labor-market effects, board oversight, fintech, cities, and private markets.

Glenn's work starts from operating and investing experience. He has priced risk on a Wall Street trading floor, invested venture capital, built and sold digital-media businesses, taught at NYU and CUNY, and now serves as chief financial officer at SoLo Funds. His writing asks what new technologies cost, whether they produce measurable value, how they change work, and what leaders should demand before believing the headline.

His analysis and commentary have appeared in or been featured by TechCrunch, Fortune, TIME, L'Express, Adweek, and Mexico Today.

Speaker

Jamaal Glenn is a speaker, moderator, and commentator on the business decisions shaping finance, technology, and work. Drawing on his experience as a chief financial officer, investor, entrepreneur, board director, and former NYU and CUNY professor, he turns abstract debates into concrete questions about cost, value, risk, governance, and execution.

He speaks on fintech and the future of finance, AI and the future of work, local news, and board governance. He has spoken and moderated for the Council on Foreign Relations, the German Marshall Fund, universities, conferences, and podcasts.
